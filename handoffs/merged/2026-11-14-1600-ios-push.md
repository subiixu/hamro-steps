## What the user asked
(T-7, T-9) Wire iOS push end to end against `ActivityService`: aps entitlement, APNs token → `registerDevice`, permission at the right moment, unregister before sign-out/deletion, and route notification taps to the right family on Home.

## What changed
- **Entitlement:** `aps-environment: development` in project.yml (XcodeGen regenerated `HamroSteps.entitlements` with it).
- **Permission:** the app never asks before sign-in. The prompt (alert, sound, badge) shows the first time Home appears, which means signed in and in a family. If the person already allowed notifications (including provisional or ephemeral), every launch while signed in registers again without prompting, even during onboarding. If they denied, nothing is registered.
- **Token:** `didRegisterForRemoteNotificationsWithDeviceToken` → lowercase hex → `registerDevice(…, .sandbox)` in DEBUG and `.production` otherwise.
  - The phone remembers every token that may have reached the backend for the signed-in account, in UserDefaults `push.deviceTokens`. A token is added before its call is queued.
  - A token that arrives after sign-out is ignored.
- **One serial queue:** `PushQueue`, an actor, runs one job at a time, each to the end, in order. It owns:
  - every `registerDevice` and `unregisterDevice`;
  - the whole sign-out, including `auth.signOut()`;
  - sign-in;
  - account deletion.

  So a job for account A always finishes before account B's sign-in starts. Nothing is abandoned on a timeout.
- **Request timeout:** each device call has its own timeout (10 s). On timeout the call is cancelled and waited for until it ends, so it can't land later, behind the next job.
- **Sign-out / account switch:** `SessionStore.signOut()` queues one job: unregister every remembered token (errors and timeouts ignored), then `auth.signOut()`, then forget the tokens. A sign-in started meanwhile queues behind it.
  - If the app quits mid-sign-out, a flag (`push.signOutPending`) makes the next launch finish it, as that account, and start signed out.
- **Delete:** `deleteAccount()` queues one job: unregister every token, then delete.
  - On failure the person stays signed in and the phone registers again (`restore()`). That registration queues behind the cleanup, so the cleanup can't undo it.
- **Tap routing:** the app delegate is now the `UNUserNotificationCenterDelegate`. Foreground pushes show banner + list + sound.
  - A tap parses `{type, familyId, eventId?}`, switches to the Home tab and selects that family.
  - An unknown type, a missing `familyId` or a family the person isn't in leaves Home unchanged.
  - On a cold start or during sign-in/onboarding, the tap waits in `PushInbox` until Home is showing with its family list.
  - Home has no hook to scroll to a feed event, so `eventId` is parsed and carried in `PushRoute.family(id:eventId:)` but not used yet.
- The logic lives in small types under `ios/HamroSteps/Push/`: `DeviceToken`, `DeviceTokenStore`, `PushRegistrar` + `PushQueue` (prompt timing, the queue, sign-out/deletion cleanup) and `PushRouter`/`PushInbox`. The delegate and RootView glue stays thin.
- Today this runs against the mock activity service from `AppLaunch.services()`. Real registration needs the Convex `ActivityService`.

## Redo (round 4)
Jarvis's design change, for review findings 1 and 2 of handoffs/reviews/2026-11-14-1600-ios-push-second.md (round-4 opinion):
- **One serial queue owns everything:** `PushQueue` (actor, in `Push/PushRegistrar.swift`) runs every register and unregister, the whole sign-out including `auth.signOut()`, sign-in (`PushRegistrar.signIn`, used by `SessionStore.signIn`) and account deletion (`PushRegistrar.deleteAccount`). Jobs never overlap.
  - The next sign-in is itself a job, so it starts only once the queue ahead of it is empty.
  - Nothing is abandoned on a timeout.
- **Per-request timeout instead:** `PushRegistrar.withTimeout` races the call against a sleep in a task group. On timeout it cancels the call and waits for it to end before returning.
  - So a registration started for A finishes or is cancelled before any job for B starts.
  - The queue can only hang if a device call ignores cancellation. The Convex connection maps `CancellationError` (`ConvexConnection.swift`), and the future Convex `ActivityService` must keep honouring it.
- **Finding 1 (A's late registration taking the token back):** can't happen any more. B's sign-in waits behind A's registration, A's cleanup and A's `auth.signOut()`.
- **Finding 2 (a slow logout overtaken by the next sign-in):** the sign-in job can't start until the sign-out job, `auth.signOut()` included, has returned.
- **Deleted:** the 3 s sign-out cap, `run(timeout:)`/`ResumeOnce`, the generation counter, `settle()`, `prepareForDeletion()`/`accountDeleted()`, `finishSession`/`endGeneration`, and the abandon/skip logic.
- **Tests (suspendable fakes):** `RecordingActivity` can hold registrations for one account only (`gatedAccount`). `RecordingAuth.signOutGate` holds `signOut` before it clears the session, like the Convex auth service does.
  - `theLastAccountsRegistrationFinishesBeforeTheNextAccountRegisters`: A's registration is held, then A signs out and B signs in. Nothing runs until A's registration is released. The log is then `register cd as u_me, unregister cd as u_me, signOut u_me, signIn u_other, register cd as u_other`, and B owns the token.
  - `aSuspendedSignOutCannotBeOvertakenByANewSignIn`: `auth.signOut()` is held and a sign-in is started. The sign-in waits, and afterwards the backend is signed in as `u_other`, not cleared.
  - `aHungRequestIsCutOffByItsTimeout`: two hanging unregisters are cancelled at 200 ms each, and sign-out completes in under 3 s.
  - `withTimeoutReturnsTheResultInTime`.
  - Kept, adapted to the queue: in-flight registration unregistered after it; every token (A held, B queued) unregistered; failed deletion re-registers after the cleanup; unfinished sign-out finished at next launch; unregister-before-signOut/delete; signOut despite unregister failure.
- **Behaviour change for the person:** sign-out now waits for the cleanup instead of capping at 3 s. It's bounded by the request timeout per remembered token (10 s each; normally there's one) plus `auth.signOut()`.
